Three levels: workspaces, panels, terminal

The terminal client works at one of three levels, and the keys mean what that level is about. ↵ goes in, esc comes out, and the status bar always says where you are and what each key does. Click the terminal or tab into it. ? lists the keys of the level you are in.

Level 2 · PANEL (the panels of the workspace). h/j/k/l change panel, ↵ starts typing in it. n makes a new panel, r names the panel, f shows it full screen, x closes it. esc goes back to the workspaces. Workspace commands that do not clash (o w s d) work here too, and so do a avisos, m phone and , settings at both levels.

  3. Level 3 · TERMINAL (typing). Everything goes to the program, esc and ctrl keys included (nvim, claude and the shell use them). The only way out is ^␣, which takes you to the panel level; so the old muscle memory still works: ^␣ x, ^␣ n, ^␣ m are the key you would press at level 2.

  4. Where you are is always visible: the chip (WORKSPACE, PANEL, TERMINAL), a blue border on the active area only, and the keys of the level on the status bar. ? opens the whole list of the level.
