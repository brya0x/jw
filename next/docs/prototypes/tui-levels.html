Three levels: workspaces, panels, terminal

The terminal client works at one of three levels, and the keys mean what that level is about. ↵ goes in, esc comes out, and the status bar always says where you are and what each key does. Click the terminal or tab into it. ? lists the keys of the level you are in.

  1. Level 1 · WORKSPACE (the sidebar is active). j/k move, ↵ goes into the workspace. r renames it, x closes it (and d in that question removes a worktree, as today), o opens a folder, w makes a worktree, s syncs it, d opens its changes. q quits.
    2. 
      
  2. Level 2 · PANEL (the panels of the workspace). h/j/k/l change panel, ↵ starts typing in it. n makes a new panel, r names the panel, f shows it full screen, x closes it. esc goes back to the workspaces. Workspace commands that do not clash (o w s d) work here too, and so do a avisos, m phone and , settings at both levels.
    3. 
      
  3. Level 3 · TERMINAL (typing). Everything goes to the program, esc and ctrl keys included (nvim, claude and the shell use them). The only way out is ^␣, which takes you to the panel level; so the old muscle memory still works: ^␣ x, ^␣ n, ^␣ m are the key you would press at level 2.
    4. 
      
  4. Where you are is always visible: the chip (WORKSPACE, PANEL, TERMINAL), a blue border on the active area only, and the keys of the level on the status bar. ? opens the whole list of the level.
    5. 
      
  5. The mouse goes straight in: a click on a workspace opens it (panel level) and a click on a panel starts typing in it (terminal level).
    6. 
      
  6. The list is grouped: each repository's root, then its worktrees indented under it (↳, the dot and the name moved four columns in); a worktree whose root is not open sits under the repository's name; the folders that are not repositories come last under carpetas. The numbers and j/k follow what is drawn; headings are not highlighted. The same grouping is on the phone.
    7. 
      
  7. Panel with a program: n keeps making a shell. N (shift n; from the terminal level that is ^␣ N) opens a menu, «panel nuevo», with shell first and then your programs. j/k and ↵, or the program's own letter, open it in a new panel; esc cancels. The panel is still a shell that runs the program first, so when the program ends you land at a prompt and the panel stays.
    8. 
      
  8. The config is a file: ~/.config/jwn/config.toml with one [[program]] per entry: name, command (a list: the program and its arguments, no quoting to get wrong) and an optional key (one letter; j k n are taken by the menu, and a repeated letter is ignored). The menu follows the file's order. The terminal client reads it and never writes it, and re-reads it every 2 s like settings.json, so editing it is enough. The daemon never sees it: the client sends new_pane with the program it already has.
    9. 
      
  9. Defaults and errors: with no file, or none with a [[program]], the menu has claude; your entries replace that list, they do not add to it. If the file does not parse, the menu keeps the last good list and says what is wrong and on which line, in red, inside the menu. Try the three states above.
